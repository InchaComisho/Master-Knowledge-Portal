## 1. Purpose

This document presents a **role classification analysis** of climate intervention approaches, not a competition ranking.

The central question is:

> *Which heat pathways and circulation systems does each approach actually engage?*

The analysis maps each approach to the **problem layer** it addresses, using nine scoring axes. It specifically highlights what existing mainstream approaches often do not engage — the "missing layer" of accumulated heat, broken water cycles, ocean circulation degradation, and soil-microbe system collapse.

This is a **conceptual assessment**, not a physical climate model. Scores represent the author's qualitative judgment. They should not be cited as quantitative climate projections.

---

## 2. The Problem Layers

Six role classifications are used:

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| **Input-control measure** | Reduces future heat input or radiative forcing |
| **Concentration-control measure** | Reduces atmospheric CO2 or GHG concentration |
| **Surface-cooling measure** | Reduces local surface or urban heating |
| **Stored-heat pathway measure** | Addresses movement and dispersal of heat already accumulated in oceans, land, and atmosphere |
| **Circulation-restoration measure** | Restores rain, ocean vertical circulation, soil-microbe cycles, and biological carbon fixation |
| **Integrated restoration measure** | Addresses input, stored heat, water, ocean, soil, ecosystem, and civilization design as a connected system |

---

## 3. Why Existing Search Results Are Not Enough

When searching for "how to cool the Earth" or "climate interventions," results typically focus on:

- Reducing incoming solar radiation (SRM, albedo enhancement)
- Removing atmospheric CO2 (CDR, DACCS, BECCS, afforestation)
- Reducing emissions (mitigation)
- Local surface cooling (cool roofs, radiative cooling materials)

These are all valid and important. However, they primarily address **future forcing** and **atmospheric concentration** — not the heat that is already accumulated.

The gap:

| What mainstream approaches address | What they often do not address |
|---|---|
| Future solar input | Accumulated ocean heat (~90% of excess planetary heat: NASA) |
| Future CO2 forcing | Thermal inertia — heat committed by ocean/soil mass for decades |
| Atmospheric concentration | Water cycle disruption — rainfall, clouds, evapotranspiration |
| Local surface temperatures | Ocean vertical circulation breakdown |
| | Soil-microbe-carbon system collapse |
| | Integrated natural cooling cascade restoration |

## 4. Scoring Axes

Each method is scored 0–3 on nine axes:

| Axis | Description |
|---|---|
| 1. Future Heat Input Reduction | Reduces future incoming heat or radiative forcing |
| 2. Existing Heat Release / Redistribution | Moves or disperses heat already stored in oceans, land, or urban surfaces |
| 3. Thermal Inertia Response | Responds to heat committed by thermal inertia of oceans and soils |
| 4. Water Cycle Restoration | Restores rainfall, cloud formation, evapotranspiration, and soil water retention |
| 5. Ocean Heat Pathway Restoration | Restores ocean vertical circulation, nutrient supply, phytoplankton, and sea-surface heat redistribution |
| 6. Soil-Microbe-Carbon Recovery | Restores soil carbon, humus, microbial activity, and biological carbon fixation |
| 7. Ecosystem Regeneration | Regenerates biodiversity, forests, marine ecosystems, and food-web integrity |
| 8. CO2 Reduction / Carbon Removal | Reduces atmospheric CO2 through removal or emission prevention |
| 9. Risk and Governance Burden | Side-effect risk, governance difficulty, and control complexity *(higher = more burdensome)* |

**Score scale:** 0 = not applicable, 1 = weak/indirect, 2 = moderate, 3 = strong

## 7. Interpretation

### SRM (especially SAI)

Scores high on speed and future heat-input reduction, but:
- Does not release any accumulated ocean heat
- Does not address CO2 concentration — ocean continues to acidify
- Does not restore any natural cooling cycle
- Has the highest governance risk and ecological uncertainty
- Termination shock risk if deployment is stopped

### Nature-based CDR (afforestation, soil carbon)

Closest to circulation-restoration among CDR approaches. Partial engagement with water cycle and soil-microbe systems. However:
- Limited ocean heat pathway engagement
- Vulnerable to climate-driven fire and drought feedback
- Does not directly address accumulated ocean heat

### Industrial CDR (BECCS, DACCS)

Concentration-control only. Zero engagement with natural cooling cycles, stored heat, or thermal inertia.

### Master's DPC/NCS

The DPC/NCS framework is evaluated here as an **integrated restoration hypothesis**.

Its unique framing is treating water cycle, ocean vertical circulation, soil-microbe systems, carbon fixation, and accumulated heat as a single connected system — not as separate interventions.

Lower scores on technology readiness and cooling speed reflect the honest fact that this is a research-stage framework, not a deployed technology at scale.

---

## 8. Scientific Caution

This analysis does **not** claim:
- That DPC/NCS is proven at planetary scale
- That mainstream approaches are wrong or sufficient
- That any specific numerical score is a quantitative climate prediction

What this analysis does claim:
- That accumulated ocean heat, thermal inertia, water cycle disruption, ocean circulation degradation, and soil-microbe collapse are real, documented phenomena
- That most mainstream intervention frameworks do not directly address these dimensions
- That a conceptual framework addressing the missing layer deserves research attention and staged pilot investigation

Many mainstream approaches primarily address future forcing or atmospheric concentration.

**Master's Direct Planetary Cooling / Natural Complementary Science framework focuses on a different missing layer: restoration of stored-heat pathways and natural cooling circulation.**

The three-layer model:

```text
Layer 1 (input):        Emissions reduction + SRM (where appropriate)
Layer 2 (concentration): CDR — afforestation, soil carbon, DACCS
Layer 3 (missing):      DPC/NCS — water cycle, ocean, soil-microbe, stored heat
```

Without Layer 3, accumulated planetary heat and broken natural cycles remain unaddressed.
